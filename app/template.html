No public data counts moving jobs in Singapore, so we estimate household moves from official housing records. Every sale, new tenancy or newly completed flat means someone moving.

How we count moves

No public data counts moving jobs in Singapore, so we estimate moves from housing records: every sale, new tenancy or new flat means a household moving. These are the moves a moving company competes for, not jobs any company actually did.

TypeOfficial recordMovesWhy
HDB resaleHDB resale transactions (since 1990)2 per saleSeller moves out, buyer moves in
HDB rentalHDB approvals to rent out a whole flat (since 2007, older file)50% of approvalsSome approvals are renewals with the same tenant (assumption, slider)
New BTO flatsFlats completed per year (HDB Property Information)1 per flatOwner moves in at completion
Condo and landedURA transactions (since 2000)2 per resale, 1 per completed new saleOff-plan buyers cannot move in yet

Limits. Not every move hires a mover (some use a friend's van); our market-share assumption is a share of all estimated moves, so it absorbs this. Moves without a record are missed (moving in with family, leaving Singapore, private-home tenancies), so the true total is likely higher. Timing is approximate: resale moves happen around completion, about 2–3 months after the sale, and BTO owners often renovate before moving in.

Towns fall into a few demand profiles

Not every town moves the same way. We score each of the 26 HDB towns on five things (how often flats are resold, how fast that is growing, how many flats reach MOP soon, how much renting goes on, how many flats are 4-room or bigger) and let k-means put similar towns into groups. The groups tell us what kind of work to expect where, and a second step picks where to park the trucks.

Each bubble is an HDB town at its real location on the map; bigger bubble = more HDB flats; colour = its group. Hover for the town's scores. Black diamonds = suggested depot sites: a second k-means on the towns' locations, pulled towards towns with more estimated moves, so each depot sits close to the most work.

Own enough crews to fully cover most months

Newsvendor logic on capacity. An idle crew costs us its wages and truck (overage). A job beyond capacity goes to a subcontractor at a premium (underage). Own capacity up to the demand quantile where the two balance.

Phasing the fleet cuts the downside

Trucks are fixed assets; the market share we win is not. We simulate 2,000 five-year futures (demand, market mood, share won, price pressure, truck downtime) and run the same futures through four ways of building the fleet.
